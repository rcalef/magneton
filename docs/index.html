Greater than the Sum of Its Parts: Building Substructure into Protein Encoding Models

Robert Calef1,2 Arthur Liang1 Manolis Kellis1 Marinka Zitnik2

1MIT   2Harvard University

Paper Code Dataset

Abstract

Proteins are not monolithic entities—they possess recurrent, evolutionarily conserved substructures such as domains and functional sites that determine their function. However, current protein representation learning approaches largely ignore this intermediate level of organization, instead treating proteins as simple sequences or focusing solely on tertiary structure.

We introduce Magneton, an integrated environment for developing substructure-aware protein models. Magneton provides: (1) a large-scale dataset of 530,601 proteins annotated with over 1.7 million substructures spanning 13,075 types; (2) a framework for training substructure-aware protein models; and (3) a benchmark suite of 13 tasks probing residue-, substructure-, and protein-level representations.

Using Magneton, we develop substructure-tuning, a supervised finetuning method that distills substructural knowledge into pretrained protein models. Across state-of-the-art sequence- and structure-based models, substructure-tuning improves function-related tasks while revealing that substructural signals are complementary to global structural information.

Overview

Proteins organize hierarchically from primary sequence through secondary structure to folds, domains, and full tertiary structure. Magneton bridges the gap between sequence-level and structure-level representations by explicitly incorporating substructural annotations.

Figure 1: (A) Protein structural hierarchy from primary sequence to full tertiary structure. (B) Magneton incorporates curated substructure annotations (secondary structure, functional sites, domains) to enable substructure-tuning, improving downstream tasks compared to existing methods that rely only on sequence or full tertiary structure.

Substructure Tuning

Substructure-tuning pools residue embeddings into substructure representations and trains classifiers to predict substructure types, distilling this knowledge into the base protein model.

Figure 2: Substructure tuning architecture. Residue embeddings from a protein model are pooled into substructure embeddings based on curated annotations. Type-specific classifiers (secondary structure, active site, domain) are trained jointly, with gradients flowing back to finetune the base model.

Key Features

Large-Scale Dataset

530,601 proteins with 1.7M+ substructure annotations across 13,075 types from InterPro and SwissProt.

Flexible Framework

Easy integration with state-of-the-art models including ESM2, ESM-C, SaProt, and ProSST.

Comprehensive Benchmarks

13 evaluation tasks spanning residue-, substructure-, and protein-level predictions.

Results

Substructure-tuning achieves high accuracy across proteins with varying numbers of domains and generalizes well even for rare domain types.

Figure 3: (A) Average accuracy per protein stratified by number of domains. (B) Per-domain test set accuracy vs. domain occurrences in the training set, showing robust performance across the frequency spectrum.
